Overview

We present a framework that unifies the synthesis of different force-closure dexterous manipulation skills into a unified solution space, leading to a single policy to perform grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation. The unified policy further supports seamless chaining of different skills for long-horizon continuous manipulation. The embodiment-agnostic framework can also be effectively applied to different hand morphologies.

Abstract

Force-closure manipulation is a fundamental component of human dexterous manipulation, enabling stable yet flexible hand-object interactions. From the perspective of hand-object relative motion, this kind of manipulation can be categorized into four canonical skills: grasping, relocation, in-hand rotation, and in-hand translation. Human hands naturally involve flexible compositions of these skills to accomplish complex manipulation tasks.

However, prior approaches often model them separately with skill-specific action constraints, objectives, or even dedicated hand morphologies, which limits generalization and disrupts continuity for compositional long-term manipulation. In this work, we present a unified framework that models all four skills within a single formulation, sharing the same state and action spaces and using a common objective structure. This unified formulation enables a single policy to learn all skills jointly, achieving per-skill performance comparable to or exceeding specialized methods.

With a compact interaction-aware object shape representation, the policy performs geometry-adaptive motions and generalizes to unseen shapes. Without skill-specific constraints, the policy naturally supports seamless composition of multiple skills for long-horizon manipulation. Moreover, the embodiment-agnostic formulation can be effectively applied to different hand morphologies. Overall, our results suggest that canonical force-closure manipulation skills can be viewed as instantiations of a unified structure, leading to a common solution space with better generalization and continuity.
